# UK email and web-page localisation

Apply this same workflow to both email designs and web-page designs.

1. Inspect the selected email or web page and its text. Duplicate it beside the original, leave the original unchanged, and name the copy “UK email” or “UK web page”, as appropriate.
2. Adapt US spelling and terminology to natural UK English, preserving meaning and tone. Always replace clothing “pants” or “pant” with “trousers”, matching capitalisation. Use “colourways”, “favourites” and “delivery” where appropriate.
3. Convert product prices from US dollars to British pounds, not euros. Use approved UK prices if supplied; otherwise use the user's supplied exchange rate or obtain the latest available dated USD-to-GBP rate from a reliable source. Calculate GBP = original USD amount × GBP per USD and round once to two decimal places. Never just replace the symbol. Report the rate, date and source, and identify calculated prices as exchange-rate conversions rather than confirmed UK retail prices. If no reliable rate is available, ask for one. When correcting a previous symbol-only localisation, use the original USD amounts, not the relabelled pound amounts.
4. The US free-delivery threshold is $200; the UK threshold is a fixed £150. Use £150 in the UK delivery announcement. This is a supplied regional policy, not an exchange-rate conversion.
5. Review dates, measurements, sizing, URLs and regional terms. Use unambiguous UK date formatting where possible. Do not invent UK sizes, destinations, addresses, tax claims, legal terms or other commercial policies; flag anything requiring confirmation.
6. Preserve typography, styling, images, component links and layout. Check that longer copy remains readable without clipping or overlap.
7. Summarise the changes, link to the UK copy, and flag outstanding regional details.
